## **What is a [[Myth]]?**
- A story that has been passed down either orally or physically that describes the belief systems of the people or describes a natural phenomenon.
- The word myth comes from the Greek Word _mythos:_
- Which comes from:
	- Word
	- Speech
	- Tale and Story
	- Nothing specific about the content.
    
- How do we differentiate myths from other narratives?

- According to Basco:

|          |         |             |                                   |                   |                      |                        |
| -------- | ------- | ----------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Form     | Belief  | Time        | Place                             | Attitude          | Principal Characters | Examples               |
| Myth     | Fact    | Remote Past | Different World: Other or earlier | Sacred            | Non-Human            | Creation Myths         |
| Legend   | Fact    | Recent Past | World of Today                    | Secular or Sacred | Human                | King Author            |
| Folktale | Fiction | Anytime     | Any Place                         | Secular           | Human or Non-Human   | The Fox and the Grapes |

## **Attempting a Definition:**
- A **traditional sacred** **story** of **anonymous** authorship and **universal significance** which is recounted in a certain **community** and is often linked with **ritual**; that it tells of deeds of **superhuman beings** such as gods, demigods, heroes, spirits, or ghosts; that is set **outside historical time**  in primal or eschatological (last, ultimate) time or in the supernatural world.
- Defined by D. Cupitt
	- It excludes visual art and literacy work and work with a known author.
	- It focuses exclusively on tales dealing with the supernatural.
	- Excludes Stories like:
		- George Washington’s Myth that he cannot lie.
		- It is too closely linked with religion.
- A Better Attempt:
	- Myths can have many forms (oral, written, visual), but they must tell a story that compels readers/viewers to search out what it means.
	- The content of myths is composed of the religious, political, cultural values that connect an individual to a society or to the universe more broadly.
	- The function of myths is to offer ways of locating one’s own experience in a broader context (societal or universal).

## **Who are the Greeks?**
- People who spoke Greek (not Barbarians).
- No unified nation-state but independent city-states ([Polis](../../Terms/TermsForChapterOne/WeekOne/Polis.md))
- Shared cultural identity.
- ## Mycenaean Period (1400-1100 BCE):
	- People who spoke Greek started to develop society in the Greek Islands and Modern day Greece
	- No literature from this period.
- ## Archaic Period (800-490 BCE):
	- Rediscovery of writing after ‘Dark Ages’ (1150-800 BCE): Greek Alphabet
	- Greek Colonization of Modern Greece through the Mediterranean
	- City-states([Polis](../../Terms/TermsForChapterOne/WeekOne/Polis.md)) ruled by aristocracy (_aristoi_=the best).
	- Homeric Poems; Hesiod; Homeric Hymns
- ## Classical Period (490-323 BCE):
	- Golden Age of Athens
	- Democracy
	- Tragedy, Philosophy, History
- ## Greek Society:
	- Patriarchal Society:
	- In Athens, Only men were able to vote
	- Stories were mainly written by Men.
	- Role of the Women:
		- Home makers
		- Sometimes Priestess
		- Some powers in the Household and some in the religious.
	- _Oikos:_ Household
		- Patriarch
		- Matriarch
		- Kids
		- Slaves
	- _Polis_: City-state.
	- Slave Society:
		- Mainly prisoners.
- ## Greek Religion: Main Features;    
	- [Greek Polytheism](../../Terms/TermsForChapterOne/WeekOne/Greek%20Polytheism.md):
		- 12 Main gods (‘[Olympians](../../../../Olympians)’)
		- Countless minor deities (river gods, forest gods).
		- No central religious authorities.
		- No sacred books (Not like Christianity’s Bible, Judaism, Islam’s Quran).
		- Strong local variations.
	- Greek Religions: Main Activities:
		- Sacrifices: Crops, or Animals
		- Oracles: The main way people talk to the Gods and follow decisions (ex: Oracle of Delphi, Dodona).
		- Religious Festivals: Olympic Games.
